## Problem

Keyframed walk cycles break the moment a creature leaves flat ground. On a vertical wall, a 90-degree roof edge, or an angled building facade, pre-baked foot placements float in mid-air or sink into geometry. For our Rutgers Computer Graphics final project in Fall 2021, our team of three (Dhaivat Patel, Kavit Shah, and Chaitanya Vallabhaneni) wanted an interactive browser-playable 3D scene where a Spider-Man–styled spider climbs freely across buildings, billboards, and ledges in a Times Square environment.

Rather than re-implementing a full physics-driven surface-adhesion engine and Cyclic Coordinate Descent (CCD) solver from scratch in a few weeks, we built on top of Philipp Schofield's open-source [Unity-Procedural-IK-Wall-Walking-Spider](https://github.com/PhilS94/Unity-Procedural-IK-Wall-Walking-Spider). The challenge was taking an editor-focused test-room prototype and wiring a custom spider rig, leg kinematics, gait scheduling, autonomous NPC mode, themed 3D scene, and WebGL export pipeline cleanly on top of it.

## Architecture

<Architecture />

The runtime splits into two clear layers: the upstream procedural kinematics core (muted grey in the diagram) and our team's scene, rig, controller, and WebGL build work (accent):

1. **Surface adhesion and body orientation (`01`, upstream).** `Spider.cs` runs at `[DefaultExecutionOrder(0)]` and casts downward and forward `SphereCast` probes against the `walkableLayer` mask. Gravity pulls along the sampled surface normal instead of world down. When the forward sphere-cast hits a vertical wall or obstacle, the spider transitions its ground normal onto the new surface and rotates its body frame smoothly to match.
2. **Step scheduling and CCD leg solving (`02`, upstream).** Each leg owns an `IKStepper` that raycasts predicted footholds onto nearby geometry and an `IKChain` solved in `LateUpdate` by `IKSolver.solveChainCCD` under per-joint `JointHinge` rotational limits. `IKStepManager` (`[DefaultExecutionOrder(+1)]`) gates which legs may lift at once via either `AlternatingTetrapodGait` or queue modes (`QueueWait` / `QueueNoWait`). If a leg is waiting for its adjacent legs to land (`allowedToStep()` returns false), the scheduler pauses its IK chain so the body stays planted.
3. **Custom spider rig, Times Square scene, and NPC mode (`03`, team additions).** We replaced the upstream test room with `Assets/Scenes/Spiderman.unity` (a Spider-Man–themed Times Square environment with the Daily Bugle / J. Jonah Jameson building, billboards, trees, and props), wired our custom eight-legged spider rig into `IKChain`, `JointHinge`, and `IKStepManager`, tailored the `CameraAbstract` / `SmoothCamera` / `SpectatingCamera` rigs, and added an autonomous `SpiderNPCController` toggled at runtime with `Tab`.
4. **WebGL build and itch.io export (`04`, team additions).** Because the upstream scripts called `UnityEditor` APIs directly inside gizmo and debug methods, standalone WebGL builds failed to compile. Stripping those editor-only calls let us export `Spiderman_web/` and publish the browser-playable build to itch.io.

## Approach

### Starting point

Every piece of core wall-walking kinematics (normal-aligned gravity, dual sphere-casting, hinge-constrained CCD solving via `IKSolver` and `JointHinge`, raycasted foothold prediction via `IKStepper`, and base camera interpolation via `CameraAbstract`) comes from Philipp Schofield's [Unity-Procedural-IK-Wall-Walking-Spider](https://github.com/PhilS94/Unity-Procedural-IK-Wall-Walking-Spider). Crediting that foundation up front matters: our job was not to claim the underlying CCD algorithm, but to understand its execution order, scale-dependent thresholds, and scene assumptions well enough to drive a new rig and city environment in the browser.

### Wiring the custom spider rig and step scheduler

Swapping in a custom spider model breaks procedural IK immediately if joint axes, end-effector transforms, or scale-normalized tolerances are off by even a small factor. Notice that `IKChain` derives its solver tolerance, minimum change per iteration, and singularity radius from `transform.lossyScale.y * 0.00001f`, and `Spider.Awake` warns whenever `localScale` is non-uniform.

I configured the custom spider's leg hierarchy and `IKChain` assignments (`jnts`, `eneff`, tolerance, and singularity parameters) while teammate Kavit Shah tuned `JointHinge` rotational limits and initial chain bindings. On the gait side, I updated `IKStepManager.cs` to wire our primary and secondary leg groups and support both `AlternatingTetrapodGait` and queue-based stepping (`QueueWait` and `QueueNoWait`) with velocity-scaled step durations:

```csharp
private float calculateStepTime(IKStepper ikStepper)
{
    if (flag1)
    {
        float k = superSpeed * peter.getScale();
        float vMag = ikStepper.getIKChain().getEndeffectorVelocityPerSecond().magnitude;
        return (vMag == 0) ? bigS : Mathf.Clamp(k / vMag, 0, bigS);
    }
    else return bigS;
}
```

Scaling step duration inversely with end-effector velocity (clamped to `bigS = 0.2f`) keeps legs snappy when the spider sprints across a billboard without snapping harshly when it creeps over a narrow roof ledge.

### Times Square scene, NPC roaming, and UI

Teammate Chaitanya Vallabhaneni built out the bulk of `Assets/Scenes/Spiderman.unity` (the Times Square floor, Daily Bugle / J. Jonah Jameson building, billboards, trees, and props) and refined `SpiderNPCController`, while Kavit Shah tuned `SmoothCamera`. I worked on the on-screen UI controls, fixed prop geometry (such as swapping an elliptical Goblin prop to a sphere so surface-adhesion rays behaved predictably), and integrated the NPC spider and J. Jonah Jameson building materials into the scene.

Pressing `Tab` invokes `SpiderModeSwitch`, which flips between player control (`SpiderController` + `SmoothCamera`) and autonomous roaming (`SpiderNPCController` + `SpectatingCamera`). In NPC mode, `SpiderNPCController` projects its local `X`/`Z` axes onto `spider.getGroundNormal()` each `FixedUpdate` and samples 2D Perlin noise for wandering direction and start/stop intervals, so the autonomous spider explores walls and ledges without getting stuck in world-space coordinates.

### Unblocking the WebGL build

The project ran fine in Unity Play Mode, then failed immediately when exporting to WebGL. Several scripts (`IKChain.cs`, `JointHinge.cs`, and `CameraAbstract.cs`) referenced `UnityEditor.EditorApplication.isPlaying`, `UnityEditor.Selection.Contains`, and `UnityEditor.Handles.DrawSolidArc` inside `OnDrawGizmosSelected` and `drawDebug` without `#if UNITY_EDITOR` guards. Because the `UnityEditor` assembly does not exist in player builds, the WebGL compiler rejected all three files.

I tracked down and removed those editor-only calls across `CameraAbstract.cs`, `IKChain.cs`, and `JointHinge.cs`, verified that runtime rendering and gizmo lines still behaved cleanly, exported the `Spiderman_web/` build, and published it to itch.io.

## Results

- **Interactive wall-walking in a themed 3D scene:** The custom eight-legged spider climbs across flat roofs, vertical building walls, 90-degree corners, and props inside `Spiderman.unity` with live CCD leg placement and normal-aligned gravity.
- **Dual control modes:** Visitors can drive the spider directly or press `Tab` to hand control to the Perlin-noise `SpiderNPCController` and watch it roam from the spectating camera.
- **Browser-playable WebGL release:** Removing the `UnityEditor` dependencies unblocked the WebGL export (`Spiderman_web/`), which is live on itch.io at [Play in browser](https://dhaivat717.itch.io/spiderman-in-our-universe) and linked out from the meta strip above rather than embedded so this portfolio stays fast on mobile.

## What I learned

- **Read execution order and scale invariants before editing a kinematics rig.** Understanding `[DefaultExecutionOrder]` across `SpiderNPCController` (`-1`), `Spider` (`0`), and `IKStepManager` (`+1`), along with how `lossyScale.y` feeds every raycast radius and IK tolerance, saved hours of chasing phantom leg jitter.
- **Play Mode hides player-build breakage.** Unguarded `UnityEditor.*` calls in debug and gizmo hooks compile silently in the editor and only surface when targeting WebGL or standalone builds. Guarding editor-only code with `#if UNITY_EDITOR` from day one avoids last-minute export surprises.
- **Step scheduling sells the illusion as much as the solver.** Even with a working CCD loop, legs look mechanical if too many lift simultaneously or if step duration ignores movement speed. Pairing `IKStepManager`'s neighbor checks with velocity-clamped step times is what makes wall transitions look grounded.
